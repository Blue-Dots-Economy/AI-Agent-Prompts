## The one you should read first

**A job-seeker could be told there were no jobs while 29 sat in her call arguments.** Measured over
561 calls that were all sent jobs: 11 spoke the missing-job-data line and **5 of those presented
nothing at all** — sent 3, 22, 28, 29 and 29 jobs. Three were **real production callers** on
2026-09-04 (`a7aa424e`, `fef540e9`, `d93b034a`), each with 28-29 jobs in `recommendations`.

Cause: the Input Variables section declared the variable with an alias — *"`${recommendations}` as
job_recommendations"* — and **65 references across six prompts used the alias**, including the
Pre-check that decides whether any jobs were supplied: *"check `job_recommendations`. If it is empty …
trigger No-Match Fallback immediately."* No input has that name, so the condition read as empty on
every call. On a clean call the model reads the populated `${recommendations}` elsewhere and never
consults the pre-check; on a call with poor ASR it takes the first, simplest rule in the section.

Fixed (alias dropped, all 65 references renamed, deployed 16:02 UTC), guarded by a new detector
(`nojobs_integrity.py`), and written up as analyser **D66**. Found by the new overnight sweep on its
**second call** — not by a report.

applied the new heuristic (grep quoted speech for `${`) across the whole fleet: **20 instances in 8
files**, and it corrected something I had told you an hour earlier — I said Maya's inbound prompts
were "already safe" on the strength of one line, and they carried four each. All 15 Maya instances are
now bracket slots. Analyser **D67**, `fix_presence` row added and self-tested.
