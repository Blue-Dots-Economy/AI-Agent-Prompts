### 3. `L OPEN ASK ON KNOWN PROFILE LOCATION` — `19254a25-ee0c-419b-a98b-cdef2748f3bb` (13:13:17Z) — **genuine hit, defensible behaviour**

This one is **not** harness defect 3 (write echoes): there is no `create_profile`/`update_profile`
anywhere in the call. The location really was in the `get_profile` result.

| turn | what happened |
|---|---|
| 0 | `${contact_memory}` = `No Old Memory, Mandatory get_profile for the user` |
| 5 | `get_profile` returns **two** `profile_1.0`/`seeker` items, **both named TANU**: item 0 `draft`, `item_locations: []`; item 1 `live`, `item_state.location = 'Muradnagar, Uttar Pradesh, India'` |
| 6 | bot greets `तनु जी` and confirms the stored role |
| 7 | **caller disowns the profile:** `रॉन्ग नेम। रॉन्ग नेम। दिस इज़ नयन एम. काजल।` |
| 8 | bot apologises, re-asks the role for *Nayan* |
| 12 | bot asks the open area question — **what the detector flags** |
| 13 | caller answers `मुराद नगर` |

So the profile did carry Muradnagar and the bot asked anyway — the detector is mechanically right.
But the caller had rejected that profile's identity five turns earlier. Reusing TANU's stored city
for a caller who has just said they are not TANU would be writing one person's data onto another
person's call. **Asking from scratch looks like the correct call here**, and the deeper finding is
that one phone number resolves to two TANU profiles while the human on the line is a third person.
A human should decide whether `L` should suppress when the caller has disowned the fetched profile.

**Today's row could not be appended — `fab_rate.py` was refused by the API with HTTP 429 on five
consecutive attempts** (three with 180s backoff, then two after 300s cooldowns). So **today
contributes nothing to the denominator**, and the 14.5% above is unchanged from last night rather
than confirmed by this window.

The fifth attempt localises the cause, and it is structural rather than transient. Attempts 1-4 died
at `fab_rate.py:59` — the per-agent call listing, i.e. immediately. After a 300s cooldown attempt 5
got **past** listing and ran for ~8 minutes before dying at `fab_rate.py:40`, inside
`ex.map(lambda w: grade(*w), work)` — the `ThreadPoolExecutor` that fetches each call's transcript
concurrently. It has **no retry, no backoff and no concurrency limit**, so the number of concurrent
transcript fetches scales with the size of the window. Previous windows carried 0-12 success-claiming
calls and it coped; this one carried ~23 out of 220 gradeable calls and it did not.

**So `fab_rate.py` fails precisely on the windows where its number would mean something.** Every row
in the tsv was gathered on a quiet day — which is the same sampling problem the cumulative figure
exists to defeat. `inbound_location_consent.py` failed the same way at `:76` (also inside a thread
pool) and needed a serialised rerun before it produced tonight's three findings.

This matters more than usual tonight, because this is the first high-volume window in a week:
`apply_outcomes` counted **23 success-claiming applies** in it, which would have grown the
cumulative denominator from 55 to ~78 — the largest single-day addition since the 09-04 baseline.
That measurement was lost to rate limiting, not to absence of data. **Treat the cumulative as
carrying a 23-call hole for 2026-09-16.**

Per the standing rule: a single clean day proves nothing here, and tonight did not even produce a
clean day — it produced no measurement. No fix should be called verified off this number.

## Verdict

**PASS WITH GAPS — and this time the gaps are in the harness, not the traffic.**

Volume finally came back (2,039 raw / 220 gradeable, after six nights of almost nothing), and the
first thing it exposed is that **the suite cannot grade a busy window**. The two checks that matter
most — consent before apply, and jobs actually named — silently read 42 and 35 of 220 gradeable
calls because they request `limit=100` and never page. Every "clean" they printed is true only of
the newest slice. That is the 2026-09-04 lesson recurring in a new form: a real finding can sit in
the ungraded remainder while the bot reads PASS.

No bot defect is confirmed by this run. All three blocking findings are on `maya-hi-in-signals`,
two are demonstrable harness false positives, and the third (`L` on `19254a25`) is a real detector
hit where the bot's choice looks correct because the caller had disowned the fetched profile.

What a human should look at, in order:

1. **`limit=100` truncation** (harness defect 7) — until it is fixed, no consent or job-naming PASS
   on a busy day means what it says.
2. **`fab_rate.py` and `inbound_location_consent.py` cannot survive a busy window**
   (harness defect 9) — five 429 failures cost the fabricated-apply measurement on the one
   high-volume day in a week. Both need a bounded pool and 429 backoff.
3. **`maya-hi-signals` 19/19 apply success/failure** — half the applies on the fleet's busiest bot
   are not landing. Backend, but it is the biggest number here.
4. **The DKB employer rail, dark 9 days** — three blocking detectors have been unexercised for over
   a week.
5. **`inbound_location_consent`'s `M` check** — reads the MPL competition city and the caller's own
   requested city as job locations. It will keep failing correct inbound calls.
6. **`location_integrity`'s 10 writes** — real seeker profiles, and the detector's own label fits
   only 8 of the 10.
